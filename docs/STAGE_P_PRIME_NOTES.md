## PP11 — a zero-hit sentinel is unsatisfiable against any term the drafter's own `Must match` requires to appear in prose

**Caught repeatedly across 2026-08-24, never once costing a fix cycle and never once earning its place.** The §8e.1 USER VERIFY block routinely pairs a zero-expectation with a positive control per PB20. The recurring miss is narrower: the drafter writes a `Must match` bullet REQUIRING some term to appear in a comment or NatSpec, then writes a USER VERIFY line expecting `grep -c` on that same term to read zero. The save is correct, the grep is correct, and the prediction is impossible. **Why PB20 does not already cover it.** PB20 says a zero-hit sentinel proves nothing until the pattern's REACH on the target is measured, and prescribes pairing every zero-expectation with a known-positive control. Both halves were being followed. The reach failure here is not in the target file's pre-existing content but in the payload the drafter is about to commission — the prompt and the sentinel are written in the same breath, and the sentinel is checked against the file as it exists rather than as it is about to exist. Adding a positive control does not help: the zero still reads as one or two and still looks like a defect.

## PP12 — a `-r` grep handed explicit file arguments does not recurse, and its short result does not look truncated

**Caught at PP3.2do, 2026-08-25, one rung before the wrong number would have entered the queue as fact.** The H.1 pre-flight needed the size of the spec-side fee divergence, and the sweep was a `grep -rn` for the two fee literals over `*.md`, run from the `aumm-site` clone root and piped through `head -12`. **Why the result did not look wrong.** The usual tell for a truncated sweep is output ending exactly at the limit. Here `head -12` returned 8 lines, which reads as a complete result with room to spare; the narrowing happened upstream of `head`, in the file set, where nothing announces itself. **Why neither PB20 nor PB18 already covers it.** PB20 governs the PATTERN's reach on a target — a zero that could never have matched. **Generalization.** A measurement's universe is part of the measurement, so report the denominator beside the numerator. Pair every tree sweep with its file count, `grep -rl` piped to `wc -l` alongside the occurrence count, because an unexpectedly small file count is the only symptom a silently narrowed universe produces. ## PP4.0 — Fix conformance and protected invariants (PP-D12, part 1)

**Every fix in this workstream conforms to the canon, and a fix that makes a finding disappear by weakening a protocol guarantee is not a fix.** The canon is `aummfi-bit/aumm-site`, read from the local clone per `CLAUDE.md` §4; where code and canon disagree the canon is the authority, unless PP-D23 has adjudicated that particular divergence the other way, which is the H family's business and no other rung's. ## PP4.0 — One-shot setter taxonomy and burn state (PP-D12, part 3)

**The fifteen one-shot setters G12 enumerates are NOT one class, and the difference decides whether a fix may touch them.** Classified at HEAD by asking, of each setter, whether its TARGET holds the HOLDER as an `immutable`. SIX are CYCLE-BREAKERS, where the answer is yes and the pair could not be deployed at all unless one side were seated after construction: `AuMM.setMinter` against `AuMMMinterRouter.AUMM`; `EmissionDistributor.setMintRouter` and `BodenseeBootstrapChannel.setMintRouter` against that same router's `EMISSION_DISTRIBUTOR` and `BOOTSTRAP_CHANNEL`, the router being a three-spoke hub whose three setters exist solely to close it; `GaugeEligibility.setGaugeRegistry` against `GaugeRegistry.gaugeEligibility`; and both `SwapAndDepositToBodensee` setters against `VaultClassRegistry.helper` and `GaugeRegistry.swapAndDeposit`. SEVEN are ORDERING-BREAKERS, where the target holds no reciprocal immutable and the setter exists only because the target is constructed later: the hook's `setIncendiaryModule` and `setEmissionRecorder`, both `CCBMultiplier` setters, `TVLOracle.setMiliariumRegistry` — `MiliariumRegistry` carries ZERO immutables, which is what settles that one and its `CCBMultiplier` sibling — and both `VaultClassRegistry` setters. TWO are NEITHER: `setGovernanceModule` seats a Safe rather than a contract, and `setAuMTContractForPool` is a per-pool binding outside the deploy graph entirely. The consequence for PP4 is asymmetric and must not be flattened: a cycle-breaker CANNOT be removed, since removing it makes its pair undeployable, while an ordering-breaker could in principle be replaced by reordering the deploy, so a fix proposing that is arguable rather than barred. Note also that the two `SwapAndDepositToBodensee` setters carry the codebase's ONLY conditional burn, each releasing `moduleAdmin` only once the other has run, so half-sealed is a reachable state and nothing on chain distinguishes it from sealed — which is C.8's Info instance, not a separate finding.

**The closure table counts `immutable` edges ONLY, and a SPENT one-shot setter is an immutable edge on the deployment that spent it.** This is the one place the table must be read twice. Burn state is per-DEPLOYMENT, not per-source: at Stage R nothing is burned yet and the whole set deploys in dependency order, so the immutable closure is the entire price. On a live generation it is not. `AuMM.setMinter` is already spent on Sepolia generation 3, so redeploying `AuMMMinterRouter` there cannot re-point `AuMM.minter` at the new router and therefore forces `AuMM` itself, and `AuMM` drags thirteen. A unit the table prices at four becomes the most expensive in the protocol, and the same inversion applies to every cycle-breaker whose burn has fired. This is not a defect in the map; it is why PP-D21 retires generation 3 rather than patching it in place, and why the generation-4 refresh is scheduled after the PP6 exit gate rather than interleaved with PP4. A rung that touches a live generation prices its change against the burn state recorded in `docs/STAGE_P_BIS_SEPOLIA_DEPLOYMENT_RECORD.md`; a rung that only changes source for Stage R prices it against the table alone.
